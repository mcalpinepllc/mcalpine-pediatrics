The claim that Dr. McAlpine was Savannah’s first Black woman pediatrician came from the client’s supplied brief and should be independently confirmed by the practice before publication. No verified public portrait was located in the accessible physician profiles; the authentic portrait used in the Meet section was supplied directly by the client on July 17, 2026. The published version is a client-requested derivative that preserves the supplied portrait while adding a stethoscope beneath the shirt collar. The non-stethoscope version is not published. All generated family and community photographs remain representational brand imagery and are not identified as Dr. McAlpine or her patients.

The client-supplied phrase **“board-eligible pediatrician” is not yet approved for publication**. The current Kaiser directory explicitly says board-certification information is unavailable, and the accessible Memorial profile does not make a board-status claim. The Kaiser listing supports only the narrower statement that Dr. McAlpine has been affiliated with Kaiser Permanente since 2008. The Memorial profile supports current pediatrics specialty and the Eisenhower Drive office details.

The official NPPES record supports the NPI, pediatrics taxonomy, address, telephone, fax, and Georgia taxonomy license-number field. Its “active” status describes the NPI enumeration record; it must not be presented as independent confirmation that the Georgia medical license is currently active. Current license status requires a direct Georgia Composite Medical Board verification.

The client supplied a claim of more than 30 years of global-health service in Thailand, India, Senegal, Honduras, South Africa, and other countries. The accessible official Medical Wings pages confirm that the organization began in 1998, which is 28 years before July 2026, and currently document Honduras, Colombia, and Peru missions. They do not identify Dr. McAlpine or substantiate the complete country list. Accordingly, the public website should either attribute the broader statement to the practice (for example, “The practice describes…”) or publish a narrower verified statement until the practice supplies an archival biography, résumé, or mission record.

A final July 22, 2026 search of accessible hospital, university, historical-society, and professional-profile sources did not produce independent institutional confirmation of the Emory residency dates, Mercer or Medical College of Georgia teaching dates, the complete Medical Wings service history, or the “first Black woman pediatrician” distinction. The released copy therefore attributes the historical distinction and global-service account to the practice and attributes the residency and teaching details to Dr. McAlpine’s publicly indexed professional profile. This completes the verification review without converting unconfirmed claims into independently sourced facts.
